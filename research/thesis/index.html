Hallucination-Aligned Visual Negative Construction for Multimodal DPO

Can the mistakes a model makes become a better training signal? This thesis explores visual negatives tailored to a model’s own hallucinations.

Xin YuDI40001 DIICSU Honours Project · Supervisor: Yang GaoMultimodal preference optimization

The central idea

Turn hallucinations into
targeted visual contrasts.

Generic visual perturbations may not reflect the semantic mistakes made by a vision-language model. This project uses model rollouts and semantic difference extraction to guide negative image synthesis, then filters and verifies the resulting preference pairs before cross-modal DPO training.

Limitations & context

Promising results.
Open questions.

The thesis reports one epoch of full-parameter Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct training on 8 × H100 GPUs, using DeepSpeed ZeRO-2, an effective batch size of 64, a learning rate of 5 × 10⁻⁷, and DPO β = 0.1 (Table 2). MMHal-Bench responses were scored with Qwen3-8B (Section 4.11), so these figures should be read within this evaluation setup rather than assumed directly comparable with other judge protocols.

The improvement is benchmark-dependent: Object HalBench CHAIRs increased from 19.7% to 24.3% (lower is better), while recall increased from 57.9% to 64.8%. The method does not improve every hallucination metric. Generation and judge errors can propagate; dataset size and token weighting were not fully ablated, and generalization beyond the evaluated model and visually depictable errors remains unestablished.
